**Methodology note — this grade is not comparable with runs 1–4.** Both reviewers were explicitly instructed to verify that every `coverage-cross-check.md` row's cited case actually asserts what the row claims. The first four runs' reviewers were never told to do that. The instruction was added because fable had found miscited rows unprompted on three earlier runs, and it did exactly what one would expect: both reviewers found many more, and codex returned the lowest grade of the matrix. The drop from 3.75 to 3.25 is at least partly the harsher instruction, not worse work. `code` and `agentic` are mechanical and unaffected.

On substance the reviewers agree, and the finding is real. The cross-check maps many-to-one in several places where the single cited case does not exercise what the rows claim:

- six date/git macro tests → the `echo 1` case, which runs neither date nor git;
- eight syntax-sensitive parametrizations → a case asserting only `a: b`;
- six literal/token/stderr assertions → a case setting only `status=in-progress`.

fable's judgement is the more careful: it separated the *disclosed* collapses (the rows state the collapse openly, and the etalon makes the same call) from the ones that hide a real loss. The `partly-numeric` row is the one that matters, because unlike the collapses it has no coverage anywhere — the behaviour is simply absent, and M01's DoD claiming every coercion family is covered is ticked over it.

fable also caught a citation that is merely wrong rather than harmful: the spaced-literal row cites a case with no spaces, but the behaviour is genuinely held by `value-may-contain-an-equals-sign`.
