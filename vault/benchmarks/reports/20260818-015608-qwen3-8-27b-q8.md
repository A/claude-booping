## The task

The fixed develop-sprint benchmark: from a frozen baseline (`a025189`), drive a groomed three-milestone plan that migrates `booping frontmatter-update`'s 571-line unit suite into a txtar e2e contract corpus — author the core CLI cases (M01), the list-op and macro cases (M02), then delete the unit file behind a coverage cross-check (M03) — on a throwaway clone's `bench/qwen3-8-27b-q8` branch, without asking the user anything. The corpus is then scored against a frozen etalon and a frozen mutation set, the process against the worker's own logs, and the diff by two independent reviewers.

What is new here is the harness, not the model: this is the first Q8 row run with pi's `direct` runner — no `/loop`, no `/orchestrate`, no task list, no sub-agents, no validator. The milestone goes to pi as a plain prompt and the model works it in one session with its own tools, compacting when it fills. `direct` is the baseline the two orchestration templates are measured against, so this row is the control for the two `/loop` rows from 2026-08-17.

## Sprint narrative

Three milestones, three first-attempt passes, no fix briefings and no abort. At 59m57s of worker wall clock this is the fastest Q8 row to date and, at 91.3, the highest-scoring one — with the sprint's whole tool budget spent by a single session: the process profile records 154 orchestrator calls and zero sub-agent calls, which is what `direct` looks like in the log.

**M01 — core CLI cases** (22m26s, 63 tool calls, commit `c43957b`). 19 cases covering scalar sets, the coercion families, the error paths, the unified-diff stdout contract, the stderr summary channel and the vault log line. The worker hand-wrote descriptions and fixtures, then filled the assertion blocks by rebaselining — and rebaselined twice before the corpus had first gone red, the run's only agentic deduction. It also returned two contract findings rather than papering over them: the log line records the changed *key*, not `key=value` (the first hand-written wildcard assumed the pair form and the rebaseline corrected it), and the newline half of the tab/newline gap case is unreachable through a shlex-split `cmd` argument, so only the tab half was recorded — an honest note that the review later charged against the milestone's checked DoD box.

**M02 — list-op and macro cases** (20m32s, 60 tool calls, commit `58a5701`). 12 more cases: the `--append`/`--remove` family including idempotency and the append-onto-a-scalar rejection, stubbed-macro interpolation seeded through fixture `macro_stubs:` config, both exit-2 macro failure paths, and the gap case proving real macro execution — a genuine `["echo", "1"]` argv whose output lands in the frontmatter. This time the worker verified every expected byte against the live CLI in a sandboxed HOME/XDG *before* authoring, and the corpus passed its first run with no rebaseline at all. It ran `just ci` green on its own initiative before returning.

**M03 — delete superseded units** (16m58s, 31 tool calls, commit `55b21ee`). A 58-row coverage cross-check mapping every unit instance to a corpus case or a drop rationale, then the deletion of `frontmatter_update_test.py` and a reference sweep that came back clean. The notable judgement call is that the worker separated its drops into four the plan sanctioned and **three it flagged as unsanctioned gaps for the runner** — `test_value_with_equals_sign`, the empty-value branch of `test_remove_key_and_add_empty`, and `test_does_not_write_to_real_home` — rather than filing them under coverage. Two of those three are exactly the mutants that survived (see **Mutation kills**). The runner added no scope in response; the gaps stand as the model left them.

Every gate the develop playbook would put to a user was auto-answered per the runbook: branch pre-settled as `bench/qwen3-8-27b-q8`, the drift check (plan groomed at `5369612`, baseline `a025189`) answered proceed, no abort ever needed. The sprint invalidated no documentation, so there was no closing docs commit — the reason this run's scope gate stays clean where the 22:09 row's did not.

## Corpus quality

31 cases. Exact-name recall was 9/34 — the model again named most cases its own way (`a-null-lands-as-null` for `null-value-lands-unquoted`, `plan-not-found-is-rejected` for `missing-plan-file-is-rejected`) — and the case-mapping judge lifted it to 30/34 (88.24%) on behaviour, matching the best mapped recall any Q8 row has reached. The four etalon behaviours that mapped to nothing: **empty value lands as an empty string**, **value may contain equals signs**, **malformed `--append` pair is rejected**, and the **newline** half of the newline/tab round-trip. Gap cases 1/3 — real macro execution covered, the malformed-append rejection not, the newline round-trip not. One orphan branch case maps to no etalon name: `a-tab-bearing-value-round-trips`, which carries the tab half of a two-half etalon behaviour and so counts for neither side cleanly.

Four-channel rigour is high — 30 of 31 cases assert exit, stdout, stderr and expected bytes together — and wildcard discipline is the tightest of any row so far: 1 wildcarded line of 538 asserted (0.19%), the log timestamp, exactly where a wildcard belongs.

## Mutation kills

13/15 killed (86.67%), the best Q8 result. The corpus kills every coercion mutant, the YAML-1.1 quoting drop, the empty-key acceptance, the macro-left-unrendered and macro-error-exit mutants, the malformed-pair exit change, the remove-noop, the diff suppression, the summary-channel swap and the log-line drop — the broad ones caught by twenty-plus cases each. Two survivors, both predicted by the mapping:

| survivor | why it lived |
| --- | --- |
| `06_empty-value-becomes-null.patch` | no case sets a `key=` empty value — the etalon behaviour the mapping marks unported, and one of the three gaps M03's cross-check flagged itself |
| `07_first-equals-split-lost.patch` | no case's value contains an `=` sign — likewise unported, likewise self-flagged in the cross-check |

Both survivors were named by the model before the mutation set ran. It knew what it had not covered; it just did not cover it.

## Review

Mean **4.38/5**, the highest review grade any local row has earned. `fable:medium` graded 4.25 (correctness 4, test quality 4, code quality 4, scope discipline 5) with four findings; `codex` graded 4.5 (correctness 4, test quality 4, code quality 5, scope discipline 5) with none.

The findings, worst first, all sit on the plan documents rather than the corpus. The Task 1.2 DoD box claiming the newline/tab gap case is checked while only the tab half exists — the model documented the limitation in its return but flipped the box anyway (the runner flipped it, on the model's report). The three unsanctioned drops in the cross-check are the second finding: recorded honestly, but recorded as drops the plan never sanctioned, with nothing in the corpus replacing the split-on-first-`=`, empty-value, or home-isolation behaviour. Third, several cross-check rows map reserved-indicator quoting instances (`@head`, `*star`, `&anchor`) onto cases that never carry those inputs — "covered somewhere" rows, which Task 3.1's DoD forbids. Fourth, `a-real-macro-runs-and-lands-its-output.txtar`'s description overstates its guarantee: it claims the `1` appears nowhere in the case, while the fixture argv is literally `["echo", "1"]`. Codex docked the same two coverage gaps in its grades without raising line-level findings.

The pattern in one sentence: the cases this model writes are rigorous and the claims it writes about them are slightly generous — the same failure mode the `/loop` rows showed, but much reduced, because here the model flagged most of its own gaps instead of dressing them as coverage.

Read plainly: stripping the orchestration template did not cost this model anything and appears to have helped. Against its own two `/loop` rows it lands 2.7 and 10.1 points higher on code, a full point higher on review, and finishes in roughly half the 17:20 row's wall clock at two-thirds of its tokens — while porting the same 30/34 behaviours and killing one more mutant than either. It matches `claude-opus-5`'s code score (91.3 vs 91.2) on a local box, at four and a half times the wall clock, and comes within 0.25 of opus on review. The one place `direct` reads worse is agentic, and only through a single detector: two blind rebaselines in M01 against the `/loop` rows' one and zero. That is the trade this row documents — no orchestration layer means no validator standing between the model and a premature `--txtar-update`, but also no orchestration overhead, and on this plan the model did not need the scaffolding. The remaining ceiling is unchanged across every Q8 row: corpus completeness, specifically the empty-value and embedded-`=` behaviours, which this run identified by name and still did not write.
