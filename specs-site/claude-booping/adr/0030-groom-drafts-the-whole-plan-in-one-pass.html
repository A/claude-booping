    Context§

    The current groom playbook (V0) spends 13–23 minutes and $4.25–5.62 per plan across many detached steps. A benchmark in booping-benchmarks/groom (idea/0003) ran variants on six past tasks in isolated environments; round 2 judged 12 plans per task (V2, V3, V3a, V5 × 3) on s1 (small), m2 (medium) and c1 (complex) with codex and a neutral claude -p, no rubric and no final solution, plus a mechanical incidents metric (what a plan would have prevented of what really went wrong). Each variant writes the whole plan as one file.

    Variant Mean judge score (6 cells) Incidents s1 / m2 Cost / time per plan
    V3 — template + research → draft → self-review 6.7 73% / 70% $3.09 / 9.1 min
    V3a — V3 without self-review 6.6 72% / 62% $2.75 / 7.9 min
    V2 — template only 5.8 70% / 62% $2.92 / 8.9 min
    V5 — V3 + 5 rules kept from the old groom 5.3 65% / 52% $2.62 / 7.1 min

    Only the staged variants found m2's hidden defect (the gateway truncates a take at 60 s, confirmed against the code by codex and Gemini); spread inside one variant (V3 scored both 9 and 4 on s1) is larger than between V3 and V3a. A rule ablation on V3 (45 rules in 9 batches, 40 runs, groom/ablation/report.md) found 16 rules the model follows without being told, 16 carried by the template's sections, and 9 milestone-shape rules it misses even when told (36–58%); removing a batch does not change the cost per plan.

    Criteria§

    • Plan quality at least that of V3 on the benchmark tasks.
    • Cost and wall time well under V0's.

    Decision§

    Groom is one drafting pass on the plan template with two stages, research then draft, and no separate self-review step (V3a). The 16 rules the ablation shows the model follows unprompted and the anti-pattern list leave the prompt; the template's sections stay. The draft is the whole plan in one file, milestones included; how it becomes milestone files is adr/0031. The 9 milestone-shape rules the model misses even when told are not repeated in the prompt either: they are checked after the split (adr/0031).

    V3a: research then draft§

    chosencomplexity: mediumreversibility: high

    Quality of V3 at less cost and time

    The template plus a research stage over the code and a drafting stage, in one session.

    • Pros: quality on par with V3; about 2× faster and 1.7× cheaper than V0; the shortest prompt.
    • Cons: judged on three tasks with n = 3 per variant; spread inside a variant is large.
    • Complexity: medium — the groom playbook is rewritten around one drafting step.
    • Reversibility: high — the old playbook stays in git.

    V3 with self-review§

    rejectedcomplexity: mediumreversibility: high

    +0.1 points for +12% cost and +15% time

    The same drafting pass with a self-review stage at the end.

    • Pros: highest mean score, best incidents on m2.
    • Cons: the gain over V3a is inside the noise; the step costs money and time on every plan.
    • Complexity: medium.
    • Reversibility: high.

    V3 with the old groom's rules§

    rejectedcomplexity: lowreversibility: high

    Last of the four on judges and incidents

    V3 plus five rules from the current groom the one-shot variants had missed (scoped Verify, a section shape before improvising, self-contained milestones, assumption lines, a short summary).

    • Pros: names what reviewers asked for before.
    • Cons: last of the four on both judges and incidents; never found m2's defect; the run is the fastest, which suggests the rules pull effort from research into form.
    • Complexity: low.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Keep the current groom§

    rejectedcomplexity: lowreversibility: high

    Twice the time and cost; judged lower on s1 and m2

    The multi-step groom playbook as it is (V0).

    • Pros: no work.
    • Cons: 13–23 minutes and $4.25–5.62 per plan; last on s1 and m2 in round-1 judging (codex ranked it first on c1).
    • Complexity: low.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Consequences§

    • playbooks/groom is rewritten around one drafting step; its snapshot reports change.
    • The plan template states the one-file milestone format adr/0031 cuts on.
    • A light path for small, already-decided requests is a follow-up (idea/0009, corpus case s3-booping-specs-site-registries).
