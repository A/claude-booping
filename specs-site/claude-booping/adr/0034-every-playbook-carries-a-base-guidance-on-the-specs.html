    Every playbook carries a base guidance on the specs

    acceptednot accepted

    Every playbook knows what the specs are, the values, and that it reads and writes them through a subagent, applying them where they raise quality; no per-playbook scenarios.

    Context§

    Specs stay current only if they are maintained during the work and used where they help (value/0016). Today a playbook knows about them only through rule briefs injected into its steps (rule/0020, booping:rule/0001); only the specs playbook reads the format guide (adr/0024). So spec material the owner states in passing — a value before a groom, a usage scenario, a decision — stays in the transcript, and a run that decides does not know what is already settled. adr/0033 proposed a consult at named decision points; it still depended on a playbook knowing that specs exist and what they are for, and it named its points one by one. Writing a scenario per playbook ("groom does X with stories, develop does Y with ADRs") would not cover the next playbook and would not cover what the owner says outside those scenarios.

    Criteria§

    • Any playbook, including one written later, works with the specs without a scenario written for it.
    • The main context grows by a small fixed block.
    • What a playbook writes into the specs is a draft the owner confirms (value/0001).

    Decision§

    A principle, not a set of scenarios. Every playbook — core, global or project — runs with a base guidance on the specs:

    1. What it knows. What the specs are for and what each record type holds (values, rules, conflicts, ADRs, stories, ideas, references, runbooks), well enough to recognise spec material in its own work and in what the owner says; and the values of the project and global tiers, one line each, to orient on without a lookup. It does not hold rules, ADRs or other records.
    2. How it gets it. Rendered with the project context the playbook skill already renders for every run, from the specs format guide and the values' summaries; the size is fixed and checked.
    3. How it works with them.
      • It applies the specs where they add quality to its result or knowledge of decisions already made, and skips them where they add nothing.
      • It updates them as the work happens: when its work or the owner's words produce spec material — a decision, a scenario, a departure from a record, a record the work made untrue — a draft or a candidate lands in the right place.
      • Values and anything that drops or weakens a rule stay owner drafts; every other draft follows the run's autonomy mode (adr/0036).
    4. Through a subagent. Every read or write of the specs goes through a subagent, and the playbook keeps only its short answer. A departure from a record is shown to the owner, or recorded with the ref and a [clarify] marker in an autonomous run, never silently overridden.

    Where and when a playbook does this is its own judgement under this guidance. A targeted rule for a playbook is added only after a measured miss (rule/0030) — first check: re-groom corpus case s3-booping-specs-site-registries with the guidance alone.

    Base guidance for every playbook§

    chosencomplexity: lowreversibility: high

    Covers every playbook and every unscripted moment at a fixed small cost

    The decision above.

    • Pros: one mechanism for core, global and project playbooks; the model applies a principle instead of matching a scenario; the values steer every run.
    • Cons: judgement can over-fire (drafts for chatter) or miss; the subagent's draft and the owner's confirmation are the filter.
    • Complexity: low — a rendered block, the consult and capture briefs, a size check.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Scenarios per playbook§

    rejectedcomplexity: mediumreversibility: high

    Covers only what was foreseen, rots as playbooks change

    Each playbook gets rules telling it which records to read and write at which step.

    • Pros: precise where foreseen.
    • Cons: every new playbook needs its own; what the owner says outside a scenario is lost; rules multiply.
    • Complexity: medium.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Mine transcripts after the fact§

    rejectedcomplexity: mediumreversibility: high

    Late and lossy; at most a second net in retro

    A retro or nightly step reads session logs and proposes records.

    • Pros: catches what a run missed.
    • Cons: runs after the decisions were made; the context of a sentence is gone.
    • Complexity: medium.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Full registry in every session§

    rejectedcomplexity: lowreversibility: high

    Context cost rejected in adr/0033

    Every run loads the index or the records.

    • Pros: no subagent call.
    • Cons: hundreds of lines per run; rejected in adr/0033.
    • Complexity: low.
    • Reversibility: high.

    Consequences§

    • The playbook skill's project-context render gains the guidance block (format summary, the values, the read-and-write principle); a snapshot covers its size.
    • The specs playbook's guide step accepts a handed-over piece of spec material and returns a short result, so any playbook can use it as the capture subagent.
    • A check: run the capture subagent over this conversation (2026-10-16) and compare what it records with what the owner said.
